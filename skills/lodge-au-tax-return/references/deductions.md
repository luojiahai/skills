# Deduction catalogue

Every entry here still passes **nexus, apportionment, substantiation**. This file adds what each category needs on top of those three: the method choice, the evidence, and the trap.

Ask about each category the user's occupation makes plausible. The ATO publishes occupation and industry guides — search `ato.gov.au` for the user's job title plus "deductions"; they are specific and worth reading for a job you have not covered before.

## Work-related expenses

myTax labels these by name, not by number. The `D1`–`D5` codes below are the paper return's labels for the same categories — useful because most ATO guidance and every occupation guide still uses them.

### Car — `D1`

**Choose one method, per car, for the year.**

- **Cents per kilometre** — a single rate covering all running costs, capped at 5,000 work kilometres per car. No receipts needed, but the kilometres must be reasoned: a diary, a pattern of trips, a calculation. "About 5,000" is the claim the ATO looks at first.
- **Logbook** — work-use percentage from a continuous 12-week logbook, applied to actual costs including decline in value. The logbook lasts five years unless circumstances change. Higher claims, more records.

Work out both when the user has the records and take the larger.

**The trap**: home to work is private travel, even carrying a laptop, even working late, even for a second job on the same day. It becomes deductible only in narrow cases — bulky tools with no secure storage at work, an itinerant job with no fixed workplace, or home genuinely being a base of employment. Travel between two workplaces on the same day is deductible.

A vehicle carrying one tonne or more, or nine or more passengers, is not a "car" — those costs go to other work-related expenses instead, without the 5,000 km cap.

### Travel — `D2`

Airfares, accommodation, meals and incidentals on work trips away overnight. Needs a travel diary for trips of six or more consecutive nights away.

**The trap**: the private portion of a trip that mixes work and holiday is not deductible, and neither is a companion's cost. Apportion by days.

### Clothing and laundry — `D3`

Deductible: compulsory uniforms with a logo, occupation-specific clothing (chef's checks, nurse's scrubs), protective gear (steel caps, hi-vis, sun protection for outdoor work).

Not deductible: conventional clothing, however strict the dress code, and however much the user only wears it to work. Black trousers for hospitality, a suit for an office — no.

Laundry has a per-load convention — $1 a load for work clothes only, 50c where they are washed with other clothes (TR 98/5). A total laundry claim of **$150 or less needs no written evidence**, only a record of how it was worked out, and that concession survives even when total work-related expenses exceed $300. Dry cleaning and repairs need receipts regardless.

### Self-education — `D4`

Nexus needs the course to maintain or improve the skills of the **current** job, or be likely to increase income from it. A course to get a *different* job fails, however sensible the career move.

Claimable: tuition fees, textbooks, stationery, student union fees, course-related travel, depreciation on a device used for study.

**The trap** is the loan distinction, and it cuts both ways. Tuition fees **payable under FEE-HELP, a VET Student Loan, or OS-HELP are claimable** where the course itself qualifies — taking the loan does not disqualify the fee. What is never claimable is a **repayment** of the loan (HELP, VSL, SFSS, SSL, AASL), because the fee itself was already the deductible event. **HECS-HELP student contributions for a Commonwealth supported place are not deductible at all.**

There is no longer a $250 non-deductible threshold on self-education; it was abolished from 2022–23. Do not reduce a claim by it.

### Working from home — `D5`

**Choose one method.**

- **Fixed rate** — cents per hour worked from home, covering energy, internet, phone, and stationery and computer consumables. Because those are inside the rate, they cannot also be claimed separately. Depreciation on furniture and equipment is claimed on top.
- **Actual cost** — the work-related portion of each running expense, calculated. More work, sometimes more money.

Either method needs **a record of actual hours worked from home across the whole year** — a timesheet, roster, diary or spreadsheet. An estimate is not accepted, and this is the single most common reason a home-office claim is denied. Actual cost additionally needs a representative four-week diary.

**The trap**: occupancy expenses — rent, mortgage interest, rates, insurance — are not deductible for an ordinary employee working from home, however many hours they work there. They open up only in limited circumstances where a dedicated area is genuinely a place of business, which is a high bar for someone who also has an employer's office available; see PCG 2023/1. Claiming them also costs the main-residence CGT exemption on that portion of the home, so raise the CGT consequence before the deduction.

### Tools, equipment and other — `D5`

- Under the immediate-deduction threshold: claim the work-use portion in the year of purchase.
- Over it: claim decline in value across the asset's effective life, work-use portion only.

Also here: union fees and professional association memberships, subscriptions and journals, income protection insurance **premiums** — only the part covering loss of salary and wages; **life, trauma and critical care premiums are not deductible**, nor is any part of a policy paying a capital sum for injury, nor a policy held inside super with premiums taken from contributions. A combined policy is apportioned, and a payout replacing salary is assessable income when received, overtime meal allowances paid under an award, work-related phone and internet where not already inside the fixed rate, and tax-deductible seminars and conferences.

## Donations, investments and tax affairs

- **Gifts or donations** — to a deductible gift recipient. **The old $2 minimum is gone**: it was removed from 1 July 2026, backdated to every eligible gift made from 1 July 2024, so donations of any amount to a DGR are claimable in this return. The $2 minimum still applies to **political** donations. Check DGR status on [ABN Lookup](https://abr.business.gov.au/) — not every charity is one, and most social-media and crowdfunding appeals are not. A donation that bought something — a raffle ticket, a dinner, a chocolate — is not a gift, because a material benefit came back.
- **Interest income deductions** — account-keeping fees and interest on money borrowed to earn interest income.
- **Dividend deductions** — interest on a margin loan or other borrowing used to buy income-producing shares, and ongoing portfolio management fees. Brokerage is not a deduction; it goes into the CGT cost base — see [investments.md](investments.md).
- **Cost of managing tax affairs** — last year's agent fee, tax software, ATO interest charges, and the cost of a valuation or advice for a tax matter. Litigation and general financial planning are not.

## Other deductions

- **Personal super contributions** — deductible only if the user gave the fund a **notice of intent to claim** and holds the fund's **acknowledgement**, both before lodging. No notice, no deduction, and it cannot be fixed after lodgement. Check the concessional cap, and check for carry-forward unused cap if the user's total super balance allows it. Contributions the employer made, including salary sacrifice, are not claimable here.
- **Income protection premiums** if paid outside super.
- **Low-value pool**, **project pool**, **forestry managed investment scheme** — narrower; read the myTax instruction page for each.

## What is never deductible

Fines and penalties, childcare, the private portion of anything, entertainment, relocation to take up a new job, grooming and haircuts, the cost of getting a first job or a qualification to enter a field, and any expense the employer reimbursed. An expense the employer paid an *allowance* for is different — the allowance is income, and the expense is deductible on its own merits.
