# Sole trader and business income

A sole trader's business income and deductions go in the **business and professional items** schedule inside the same individual return — there is no separate business return. Net business profit is added to the person's other income and taxed at individual rates.

Start by settling three things, because each one changes everything downstream.

## 1. Business or hobby

A hobby has no ABN, no deductions, and no assessable income. The tests are intent to profit, repetition and regularity, scale, business-like records, and commercial character. Someone selling occasional items at a market is likely a hobby; someone advertising, invoicing, and doing it every weekend is not. Get this wrong in the generous direction and the losses claimed are not available.

## 2. Personal services income

**PSI** is income mainly from a person's own skills, labour or expertise — consultants, contractors, IT and trades working through their own ABN. If income is PSI and the person is **not** conducting a personal services business, the PSI rules cut off the usual deductions: no rent for a home office, no payments to a spouse for support work, no splitting income.

Escaping the rules means self-assessing as a **personal services business**, and the tests are not a flat list of four — they are two routes:

- **Route 1 — the results test alone.** Paid for a result, supplies own tools or equipment, liable to rectify defects at own cost. Pass this and the person is a PSB regardless of client concentration. Nothing further is needed.
- **Route 2 — the 80% rule plus one other test.** First the **80% rule**: no single client (with its associates) provided 80% or more of the PSI. Only if that holds can one of the remaining tests be used —
  - **Unrelated clients test** — two or more unrelated clients won through public offers or advertising.
  - **Employment test** — others do at least 20% of the principal work by market value.
  - **Business premises test** — separate premises, used exclusively for the activity.

Where 80% or more came from one client and the results test fails, the other tests are unavailable and only an ATO **determination** can help. Work this out before touching the deductions. Read the ATO's PSI pages for the current wording. Where the answer is genuinely close — a results test that turns on who bears the cost of rectifying defects, say — apply for a private ruling on the stated facts rather than picking the favourable reading.

## 3. Cash or accruals

Cash basis records income when received; accruals when invoiced. Small businesses commonly use cash. Whichever it is, apply it to both income and expenses, and stay consistent with prior years.

## Income

All business receipts, **gross of GST if not registered, net of GST if registered**. Includes cash takings, platform payments, government grants where taxable, insurance recoveries, and the value of goods taken for private use.

Cross-check the total against bank deposits and platform statements. The ATO runs data-matching over payment platforms, ride-share and delivery apps, and the taxable payments reporting system in building, cleaning, courier, IT and security services — income from those sources is already known.

## Deductions

The general rule is the same **nexus, apportionment, substantiation**, with a wider nexus: anything incurred in carrying on the business.

- **Motor vehicle** — same two methods as an employee. See [deductions.md](deductions.md).
- **Home-based business** — unlike an employee, a sole trader with a dedicated area *can* claim a share of occupancy costs (rent, rates, mortgage interest). It has a CGT cost: the main-residence exemption is lost for that portion. Say so before claiming.
- **Depreciating assets** — immediately deductible up to the current instant asset write-off threshold, per asset. The threshold and its eligibility dates change by legislation, sometimes retrospectively — fetch it, and check the asset was first used or installed ready for use inside the eligible window.
- **Trading stock** — opening and closing values, with the change flowing through to profit.
- **Wages and super** for employees; super for the sole trader themselves is a personal contribution, not a business expense.
- **Non-commercial losses** — a business loss offsets other income only if **both** gates open. First the **income requirement**: taxable income, reportable fringe benefits, reportable super contributions and total net investment losses together must be under **$250,000**. Then **at least one of four tests**: assessable income from the activity of $20,000 or more; a tax profit in 3 of the past 5 years including this one; real property of $500,000 or more used continuously in the activity; or other assets of $100,000 or more (excluding real property and some vehicles). Fail either gate and the loss is **deferred** to a future year of the same activity — it is not lost. The Commissioner has a discretion to allow the offset anyway, which must be applied for.

## GST

Registration is required at $75,000 turnover ($150,000 for non-profits), or immediately for ride-sourcing regardless of turnover. GST is reported on a BAS, not this return. If registered, income and expenses in the return are **GST-exclusive**, and the GST portion of an asset purchase does not count toward the write-off threshold.

## PAYG instalments

A profitable first year usually triggers the ATO to start PAYG instalments for the next one. Tell the user this is coming, so the following year's cash flow is not a surprise.

## Records

Five years, and for a business that means invoices issued, receipts, bank statements, the vehicle logbook, asset purchase records with dates, and stocktake figures. If these are not in place, fixing the record-keeping is worth more than anything else in this return.
