# Investments — interest, dividends, distributions, capital gains

## Interest

Every account that paid interest, gross. Joint accounts are split by ownership share, and prefill often reports the whole amount against one holder — check this. If the bank withheld TFN amounts, the gross goes in as income and the withholding as a credit.

## Dividends

Each dividend has three numbers: the **unfranked** amount, the **franked** amount, and the **franking credit**. Assessable income is all three added together (the "gross-up"); the franking credit then comes off the tax payable, and for an individual it is **refundable** — it can produce a refund even with no tax otherwise payable.

### The integrity rules — check before claiming a credit

A franking credit is only claimable where the parcel of shares satisfies both rules, tested **per parcel**:

- **Holding period rule** — the shares must have been held **at risk for at least 45 days** (90 days for preference shares), not counting the day of acquisition or the day of disposal. It only needs satisfying once per purchase. **This rule applies only where total franking credit entitlement for the year is $5,000 or more** — below that, the small shareholder exemption switches it off.
- **Related payments rule** — applies at any size, and fails where the benefit of the franked dividend is passed on to someone else.

Dividend-stripping around ex-dividend dates is exactly what this catches, so check it whenever shares were bought shortly before a dividend and sold shortly after.

**Where a parcel fails, leave those franking credits out of the return entirely** — do not report the credit and let myTax sort it out. The grossed-up dividend income is still assessable.

- A **dividend reinvestment plan** is still a dividend received, in full, in cash terms. It is also a share purchase at that price — record it as a new CGT parcel with its own cost base.
- **Listed investment company** capital gain amounts and **conduit foreign income** appear separately on the statement and have their own labels.

## Managed funds and trust distributions

Work from the **annual tax statement** (the AMIT or standard distribution statement), never from the cash that landed in the bank. One distribution splits across many labels: interest, dividends and franking credits, other income, foreign income and foreign income tax offsets, and capital gains broken into discounted, indexed, and other.

The statement also carries an **AMIT cost base net amount** which adjusts the cost base of the units up or down. Apply it — skipping it makes every future disposal of those units wrong.

## Capital gains

A CGT event happens on **disposal**, which includes far more than selling for cash: gifting, transferring to another person, a company buy-back, a takeover, and — for crypto — swapping one token for another or spending it on goods. Each disposal is its own calculation.

For each disposal, record:

1. **Acquisition date** and **disposal date** — these decide the discount.
2. **Cost base** — purchase price, plus brokerage on the way in and out, plus stamp duty, plus non-deductible holding costs. Costs already claimed as a deduction cannot also go in the cost base.
3. **Capital proceeds** — what was received, or market value where the disposal was not at arm's length (a gift takes market value, not zero).
4. **Gain or loss** = proceeds − cost base.
5. **Discount** — an individual holding the asset more than 12 months applies the CGT discount to a gain. It does not apply to a loss, and it does not apply at exactly 12 months — the period must exceed it.

Then: capital losses offset capital gains, and are applied to **undiscounted** gains before the discount is taken, which is the ordering that produces the better result. Losses left over carry forward indefinitely. **A capital loss cannot offset salary or other ordinary income.**

Where parcels of the same asset were bought at different times, identify which parcel was sold. The choice is the user's, and it materially changes the gain — pick deliberately and keep the record.

**Main residence** is generally exempt, but the exemption breaks on renting out part or all of the home, running a business from it, or being absent beyond the allowed period. Flag it and read the current ATO page rather than assuming.

**Crypto** is a CGT asset, not currency. Every trade, swap, and purchase of goods is a disposal. Exchanges do not report a usable cost base, so the user's own transaction history is the source; if it is missing, reconstructing it is the job before anything else.

Rewards divide into two treatments, and conflating them is the common error:

- **Staking rewards, and airdrops of an established token** — ordinary income at market value on receipt, declared as other income. That same market value becomes the cost base for the later disposal.
- **Initial allocation airdrops** — the project's very first distribution, where the token had never traded before. **Not income on receipt.** Issued free, the cost base is **nil**; paid for, the cost base is what was paid. The whole gain surfaces as CGT on disposal, with the 12-month discount available.

**Personal use assets** — acquired for **$10,000 or less**, both the gain and the loss are disregarded. Acquired for more than $10,000, only the *loss* is disregarded, so a gain is still taxable. **Collectables** (art, jewellery, antiques) use a $500 acquisition threshold instead. Crypto held as an investment is not a personal use asset, and the ATO reads that exception narrowly.

A **capital gains tax schedule** is required on top of the return where the current-year capital gain or loss exceeds **$10,000**. Confirm against the current-year myTax capital gains page, and note the test is on the gain or loss, not on the proceeds.

## Where the ATO already knows

Share registries, exchanges and brokers report to the ATO, and crypto exchanges report through the data-matching program. Prefill showing nothing is not evidence the ATO knows nothing. Omitted disposals are the most common individual audit trigger in this area.
